Rust for Failures

What Is This?

This is a mining expedition. One of the things I like to do as a programmer is to find a problem area, and explore it; explore the way the data lends itself to patterns. I call it making the data plastic.

When I refer to the term plastic, I am using the ol' timey definition of the word... what Merrian-Webster has relegated to the 3a and 3b spots in their definition.

plastic - adjective
   3a : capable of being molded or modeled
   3b : capable of adapting to varying conditions : pliable

I wanted to show my readers a window into how I explore and work with systems from the ground up. It's rare that you get the luxury of exploring the fundamental domain of a problem space. It's one of the attractions of working with startups. Generally, you come into something that is so far entrenched that you feel more like the guy at the end of the circus parade cleaning up shit, than someone "innovating."

The work that makes up the code in this book started as a way for me to learn Rust by exploring a domain space that interests me: poker; specifically what they call the GTO of Game Theory Optimal style of poker poker play. Generally, the way to explore this is to purchase what they call a poker solver. It helps you with the doing the calculations around the style, allowing you to get familiar with the math, and develop your own style of play around it.

Fuck that. Let's write our own.

The original goal of this book was to allow programmers at various levels to experience what it was like to pair with me. Pair programming was something that I was introduced to at a company called Pilar Technology in Columbus, Ohio. It allowed me to side along side some amazing developers and experience how they looked at programming. Each different... each unique... each valid.

I quickly discovered that my own style is radically different than anyone else that I have ever worked with. It's a style born out of being raised by a single genius, on the spectrum lesbian mother. I call it the short circuit, or as a wise philosopher once called it, "flip mode. Flip mode's the greatest."

It's hard to explain how it works. It's mainly about how it's been my experience that stakeholders have a very narrow grasp of how the systems they want to build would actually work. How the state would be leveraged in a way to bend to their will. They think they have all the answers, whereas in fact, they've come across more often than not as toxic idiots. Are news is now filled with the results of their hubris. Always bet on stupid.

That's why I say that people don't know what they want until they see it. It's why one of my core values as a developer is what I call "demo on demand." That we should ready at any time and any place to show our work.

Enter the Clankers

One note, about five years into this little exercise, agentic coding agents started entering the playing field. I started out playing with GitHub's Copilot. I found it to be mainly good at helping to fill out long, repetative code sections such as this macro to help create hand ranges. It was amusing watching it create code snippets, only for the Rust compiler to point out through linting how the code had issues. Generally, this is because Rust is in very active development, and LLMs are months if not years behind things.

Later on, I was introduced to Claude Code at work, and decided to explore its impact on how I programmed as a veteran developer. It's allowed to to establish some defensive techniques around the technology, that have allowed me to leverage the technologies in ways that I find interesting, but also concerning... feelings that I will be exploring as a part of our work through this book.

A big plus IMNSHO in all this is that I had spent years establishing the core of this project long before I had ever heard of LLMs. This has given me a foundation that acts as a counterbalance to the intentionally designed pull that these tools have baked into them, to make you dependent on their utility. Back when I worked at the 7-11, I called this the "curse of convenience." It is one of the most dangerous aspects of capitalism, and one that I at least strive to be aware of, even as my lazy ass has a delicious Taste of Tuscany calzone delivered to my house.

Creating the Developer's HANS Device

As you will soon learn following along with me, I test the shit out of my code. I test it one way, and then another way, and even then I test it some more. Agentic coding tools have given us velosity, but at the cost of safety. We're moving at hundreds of miles an hours faster than every before without a care in the world, unprepared for when our stupid asses fly through the window of our top of the line racing car. Just as it did in the automotive field, the coding field will only discover the path forward through a lot of pain and deaths. The problem is, the fucktards creating these systems don't give a flying fuck about how many people get fucked from their fuckery. That's the job for idealistic fools like you and me, who try to find a path forward, whilst dodging the slings and arrows of life. It took for a lot of race car drivers getting brain injuries before Dr. Robert Hubbard created the HANS device. It's going to take a lot of stupid for us to figure out to same.

All I can say is, the clankers will gladly help you fuck yourself, and will also gladly help you unfuck yourself, but they will not help you avoid getting fucked in the first place. That's up to us, mother-fucker.
