Rust for Failures

Introduction

Motto: Failure is a gift.

I'm 20. It's four o'clock in the morning. I'm working at the solo graveyard shift at the Colma 7-11 (where San Francisco buries their dead) when a motor home pulls up into the parking lot. A guy steps out, and walks into the store, heading over to the coffee and pours himself a cup. Approaching the register, he extends his hand out to me.

"I just wanted to thank you."

That's when I recognized him. A year before, he was pointing a gun at my face, robbing the store of $202 dollars. The police caught him a few hours later with the thanks of their K-9 unit.

It was because of me and the Daly City PD that he turned his entire light around. Now he was working as a cook in an amazing SF restaurant, and his entire life was for the better. I took his hand, laughed, and said "you're welcome. That will be $2."

Failure is a gift, if you learn from it.

Meanwhile, I was still the same loser I was for the last two years, making shit wages, stocking shelves, mopping the floors, and making sure that the coffee was always fresh. I kept working there for another year, when a man with six bullets in him, on probation, walked into the store, and pointed a semi-automatic pistol at my face as his 16 year old partner kept watch for police.

Failure is a gift, if you learn from it.

Stealing a pack of Swisher Sweets and the $247 dollars from the register, they rushed out of the door and drove away in a stolen car, which they crashed into the Balboa Bart Station shortly thereafter where they were quickly arrested.

As I stared down the barrel of the rusty gun, I didn't see my life flash before my eyes. What I did see surprised me. You see, for the last few years I had been dating this girl who was a vegetarian. So I decided to be a vegetarian too. At that moment, however, I had one thought in my mind: man, I really want a pepperoni pizza. So that day, I went out for lunch with my friend Irving, and stopped being a vegetarian. It would take me another 10 years to get my shit together.

Failure is a gift, if you learn from it.

The thing is, I've been a failure all my life. I’ve dropped out of college three times. I graduated from high school with a D average. I’ve lost every professional French Horn audition I’ve ever taken. I spent a time homeless crashing on friends couches when they would let me, and sleeping on Bart trains, parks and the San Francisco Symphony's Davies Symphony Hall musician's lounge. (I knew the security guards because I used to play in the SF Symphony Youth Orchestra. Always be nice to the people who control the doors.)

It would take me about 10 years to finally start figure it out.

My hobby Christmas Songbook website and gotten me a $350 bill from my ISP. At the time I was making $7 an hour as a walking messenger in SF. I was fucked.

Except, it turns out I wasn't. The reason I got the spike in gtraffic was because I was written up and the prestigious Yahoo! magazine, which in turn was copied in the New York Times. Within a year, I was working for a startup called XOOM.com. 28 years later I am still programming.

Turns out, that failure fast, and failing with intent creates learning systems that get stronger and they grow. Controlled failing is one of a developers greatest superpower.

I am proof positive of this. I don't have any degrees; I am completely self taught; and I am an old fuck still employed through three market crashes.

I would argue that failure is the single most powerful force in the greatest super power on the planet. The big money is on failing big. Why use your profits to improve customer satisfaction in your airline, when you can instead give yourself and your investor pals more cash with stock buybacks? Why earn profits when you can make money promising the moon as long as number go up and you get while the getting is good? Why ensure the best returns for your customers, when you can instead create dark money pools making sure that you always take the cream off the top first? Why represent people, when you can lock in a job for life and get hot stock tips and free gigs for your fam just by not getting anything actually done?

Failure is a superpower. Some choose to use it for evil, and are incredibly wealthy. Others choose to use it for good, and are incredibly poor in comparison. I choose to use it for good, building systems that I am always proud to walk away from. Stupid me.

Who am I?

I'm Christoph. I'm a professional software developer who has been working for 28 years. I got my start in the SF dot.com boom, and went on to live through three tech sector crashes. I'm 60 years old, and can still walk onto any team coding in any language and deliver value by using the techniques that I will be cover in this book. My work is in cars and planes and banks and electric charging stations and government agencies that I am not allowed to talk about, in languages that you probably hate, like Java and PHP, and .NET and JavaScript and Ruby and Python and C++ and Kotlin, and TypeScript and C. No, I didn't make bank off of one of the startups that I worked for, and TBH, I got off pretty good walking away with nothing, given how fucked several of my friends got from companies where their contributions were critical. I did make a lot of fucking money for a lot of companies, and always by doing work that I was proud of. I try to be a force multiplier. Find gaps... automate away the gaps... repeat until no longer needed... walk away smiling. I've worked with dozens of developers who could code me under the table, and I'm grateful for how much I've learned from them. But there's only one Christoph, and I'm Christoph, mother-fucker.

Why Poker?

Poker is a nice fixed domain, with clear rules that allow us to focus on code instead of research. It's a fun way to visually show code in action. Plus, I like playing and watching poker, and it's fun. I an interested in what they call Game Theory Optimal (GTO) style of play. Instead of paying hundreds of dollars for a one of those pro solver tools, I thought that it would be more fun to write my own.

NOTE: Gambling addiction is one of the plagues of our times. I am not a gembler. I just like to plunk down a few hundreds every now and then on a tournament. More often, I am watching WSOP tournaments and shows on Poker Go. Please be careful. This code is not designed to encourage you to gamble.

SPOILER ALERT

Here are a few examples of what I am talking about. When I started out on this project, I was curious about web assembly. I wanted to build my library, and see how it faired from the command line and maybe through a web page. Now, thanks to web assembly, I can create multiple purely client side tools, such as a GTO style card combination analyzer, and a client based poker table where you play against eight bots, as well as binding it to a Python or Node.JS library. These are all built with the same foundational codebase. There are many other examples that I have been playing with, including a client/server architecture where you can play against various AI bots, and a command line terminal client for those old school like me.
