Rust for Failures

Laser Swords

One of my favorite aspect of Star Wars lore is the relationship between a Jedi, and their light saber. The highest compliment Darth Vader could give to his son was this simple statement of fact: "I see you have constructed a new light saber." Their tools were sacred. Building them, mastering them was a rite of passage... a matter of life and death.

Mastery is the relationship between a person and their tools. For my mother's father, who helped put the first man on the moon, it was his protractor, compass and slide rule. For my father's father, who was a card shark, master salesman, state senator, and mob researcher, it was a deck of cards, his mastery of cross referencing media stories long before the internet, and his ability to make you feel like the most important person in the room.

How rarely do we think about the consequences of our creations from that perspective.? We are divorced from the frustrated single mother just trying to schedule a doctor's appointment for her child, or the family trusting the "autonomous" driving software to take them home safely. We live in the age of disruption... the golden age of grift. The software written by the giants before us in the mainframes of our global financial systems still powers the world. Their creators aren't billionaries because their created a tool to look at pictures of cats on a computer, or suckered states into giving them carbon credits for their gas powered "climate saving" electric vehicles, and yet every single cent of these "titans" of silicon valley owns their fortunes to those before them that built the digital highways that enable their hubris. They are the ultimate "welfare queens" hoovering up every last cent of capital on the planet.

The man who invented the spreadsheet mainly got a footnote on wikipedia for his creation of the single most utilitarian software application of all time.

In starting this journey together, I am going to share my love of the craft. I want to show you how the tools of our chosen path are the foundation that underpins everything. You can chose the path of our age, and let the coding agents do the work for you. They will leave you weak; another clone in a vaste army of replacable clones that cant hit the side of a barn, let alone rescue a princess, and are only their to make the powerful project the illusion of strength. Cannon fodder in a world where humanity's value towards humanity has never been lower.

Yet while it feels like everyone is telling you to cut corners, to get rich quick, to make that bet on the stars, the value of craft has never been higher. You see, the secret to beating the odds in a market based world, you need to clock where everyone is headed, and go in the opposite direction.

I'm not advocating that you ignore the chaotic innovations that are generative pre-trained transformers like Code Claudette and Chat Gipity. What I am advocating is that you don't become their bitch. If you don't understand the underlying why of the droids you're having do your bidding, it's you that's the droid. Replaceable. Expendible.

Release Channel

Rather than setting channel to one of the release channels of stable, beta, or nightly, we are going to specify an exact version of the toolchain. This is because

  1. Later on, when we add linting, new lints can get added, and we don't want to accidentally break the build.
  2. It's a trauma response to having one too many times where a slight difference in versions of a language created brutal nasty bugs.

Rust Version

When I started working on this project, lints were set at the top of the primary file in the project, like this:

#![warn(clippy::pedantic)]

Since Rust 1.74.0, the preferred method of setting lints is in the project's Cargo.toml file. One big benefit of this is that applies to all of the crates in the project, if you have more than one.

Let's break down the lint settings.

Pedantic Linting

First off, the pedantic lint. Here's what the clippy docs says about it:

This lint group is for Clippy power users that want an in depth check of their code.

Yes! I'm a power user 💪! Let's go!

The reason I've set priority = -1 is to make sure that these lints go first, so that if I override any of them, such as upper_case_acronyms, I am sure that my settings hold. Since the linting doesn't ensure a specific order without a priority setting, this is how you lock it in.

Expect and Unwrap

Finally, this is probably the most important Rust lint that you an set, and TBH, I am surprised that it isn't a part of the pedantic lints: expect_used and unwrap_used. Here's why:

On November 18, 2025 Cloudflare's engineering team released the following code update:

/// Fetch edge features based on `input` struct into [`Features`] buffer.
pub fn fetch_features(
    &mut self,
    input: &dyn BotsInput,
    features: &mut Features,
) -> Result<(), (ErrorFlags, i32)> {
    // update features checksum (lower 32 bits) and copy edge feature names
    features.checksum &= 0xFFFF_FFFF_0000_0000;
    features.checksum |= u64::from(self.config.checksum);
    let (feature_values, _) = features
        .append_with_names(&self.config.feature_names)
        .unwrap();

It brought down a significant portion of the internet. Can you spot why? If you're like me, as soon as you looked over this code you let out a cackle of pure glee. Nooooooo, not that?!!!!

See, unwrap() has this wonderfully nasty habit of crashing your programs when something returns Err or None. This is why you never ever ever ever use unwrap() like this and why as soon as I saw this error, and went through all my rust libraries and made sure there weren't any of them hiding in my code.

TBH, whomever wrote this code is a real boss. I know they're probably really embarassed by it, but it takes a real king to bring down the internet with a single line of code. I remember the time I DDOSed my own startup when I had our email servers email me whenever an error was seen on the site.

Failure is a gift, and this example didn't just teach the coder a valuable lesson, it taught an entire planet of Rust developers, while giving us all a good laugh.

I do have one exception to this lint: I don't enforce it in tests. I want my bad tests to fail hard, and I want it to be as easy as possible to get the the meat of what we are testing.
