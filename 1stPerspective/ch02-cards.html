Combinations

A primary tool in how these permutations become manageable enough to play games with is combinatorics. This manageable, quantifiable breakdown of seeming randomness is why playing cards are so often exployed as contextual tools in such fields as probability theory, discrete mathematics, and game theory.

WARNING: I am not a mathematician; a failing that will become more and more pronounced as we continue on with this journey.

The particular game we are focused on is Poker. There are many variations of the game, but they all start with the basic concept of each player having a hand made up of five cards. That doesn't matter if you're talking about 5 Card Draw, Stud, Omaha 8 or Better, Razz, Texas Hold'em, or Chicago.

Since the order of the cards doesn't matter in terms of the values of the individual hands, when it comes to determining the different possible hands that a player can have, we only care about the possible combinations.9

The math nerds 🤓 would notate this as:

52​C5​=(552​)=5!(52−5)!52!​=5⋅4⋅3⋅2⋅152⋅51⋅50⋅49⋅48​=2,598,96010

The game is No Limit Texas Hold'em, specifically an exploration of playing cards, and how they can be digitally programmed to reflect the different perspectives that games of poker can have.11

What makes it a challenge, is how quickly the different possibilities can spiral out of human comprehension. The mathmetician Claude Shannon, OG computer chess programmer, described what is now called the Shannon Number, 10¹²⁰, which is a conservative lower bound of the game-tree complexity of chess. Now I am not a mathmetician, but that's a lot. Chess becomes a pure force of calculation on the part of the players, which of course creates nothing but very stable geniuses.

Poker, on the other hand, is a game where you only know what you can see, what you can sense, and what you can remember. What Game Theory calls, a game of incomplete information.

There is not a more concentrated form of pure mischief than the deck of cards. Much like pasta, playing cards started in ancient China as a drinking game before spreading through India, Persia and Egypt, only to arrive in Europe in the late 14th century; the first record of which was their being banned in the city of Bern in 1367.

The modern deck that we play today in casinos is what is called the French Deck because of their choice of suits being Spades, Hearts, Diamonds and Clubs, as well as their introduction of the Queen. 🇫🇷 Vive la France! 🇫🇷

Deconstructing the Cards

In our quest to bring poker into The Matrix, we need to define what they are digitally.

If we were to define cards from a DDD perspective we would call them Value Objects. But we would also call their Ranks and Suits Value Objects, as well as their collection into a Deck or 52, or the hands each players are dealt, or the common cards sitting in the middle of the table. One of the funny things about talking with computer scientists is, given how specific their craft is, it sure can be fucking vague.

The truth is, that every domain you wrestle with has its own unique set of textures. In finance, it is the hell that is decimal places and percentages. In mail, it's the nightmare that is addresses and zip codes.

Playing cards are a fun one. I can't think of any domain where the underlying structures are so simple, and yet the results can be so complex. Maybe it's just that mathmeticians are all degenerate gamblers, but they've managed to make a simple deck of 52 cards a central player in the study in the fields of combinatorics, probability, information, game and group theory, as well as the study of the surprisingly tricky and soon to be very much in the news thanks to the fun that is quantum computing: randomness.
