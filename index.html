Rust for Failures

τέλος

Thesis: Genius is complexity manifested from simplicity.

Why is Beethoven great? Why is his 5th Symphony in c minor his most famous work?1 A four-note theme, repeated twice. Dum dum dum dummm! Dum dum dum dummmmmmmm!

You can hear it in your head. It's like a psychic time machine. It's a sense memory that connects billions of people. Why?

What tools was Beethoven working with?

You had the Classical Style. 2 A formal harmonic and rhythmic structure, framed by a selection of contrasting movements honed by decades of tradition, founded by the dances of the royal courts, made alive by an innovative use of instruments to perform the music, served to an audience of the rising European middle class starving for novel forms of entertainment.

You had the manic Viennese composer/performer, hell-bent to pave his own way regardless of public perception, his isolation magnified by his gradual loss of hearing.

It couldn't have been the premier. That was a disaster. A barely rehearsed 4-hour-long concert, in the middle of a winter storm. Yet within a few years, it was considered a masterpiece.

Dum dum dum dummm! Dum dum dum dummmmmmmm!

So, what is it?

The secret to unlocking the pure genius of Beethoven's 5th is right there in the opening.

It's a ladder. V III IV II. 5 3 4 2 ____. We're in the ke of c minor. Start at the top: G or V. Subtract 2: III or Eb . Go up one: IV or F. Subtract 2 again: II or D. It's a formula... X - (2 + 1)...

Except something is missing. V III IV II... no I. No root of the chord. No home.

From the start, Beethoven pushes us into this manic journey. Through the movements we travel through an Odyssean struggle to find home...

  1. Allegro con brio in c minor
  2. Andante con moto in A♭ major
  3. Scherzo in c minor

Da da da dummm! Da da da dummmmmmmm!

I want to go home. This feeling is infused into the music from the most basic of elements to its largest architectural structure. Three movements of struggle, until we reach the end, and what do we find?

The C Major shores of home, beaten over our heads as only Beethoven can do.

(Is having just watched Christopher Nolan's film adaptation of The Odyssey helping?)

There is no escaping this. We have reached home. There is no subtlety in how Beethoven sends us the message. However, because it's a message of notes instead of words, while it's much harder to grab onto intellectually, it's almost impossible to ignore emotionally.

Scale up to the sweeping lines, or scale down to the tiniest details, they all serve the greater purpose of the essence of what this piece is about.

The Ancient Greeks called this Telos3: the essence of a thing. My thesis of this book is that genius is how elegantly complexity manifests itself from simplicity. Atoms becoming rocket ships. Subatomic particles becoming atoms. Chains of chemical information allowing for the creation of an infinite variety of life. A simple fungus occupying hundreds of miles.

Genius is simplicity manifesting into things of great beauty and power.

You can see the patterns everywhere.

The haystacks and water lilies of Monet. The urgent offbeat rhythmic drive echoing out across millennia until James Brown screams, "take it to the bridge!"4 The contrasting call and response of Party and Ustad Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan. The slowly accelerating mania of Lucy and Ethel wrapping chocolates. Any guitar solo by Sister Rosetta Tharpe.

They are the seeds of humanity. The link us across time and space. They are the living magic of telepathy and time travel that is consiousness.

I thought this was about fucking software

I maintain that it's the fundamental reason that certain technology companies dominate our world.

One of the purest examples of this is Conway's Game of Life where a very simple set of rules on a two dimensional grid can create amazing structures.

Our tech landscape is dominated by titans who were able to translate an elegant technical idea, and create planet altering empires out of it.

Google's genius is in their MapReduce. It is the essence of everything they create. An idea that takes data, and gives the owner of that data seemingly limitless power to shape and filter. This foundation has spawned many applications, more often than not winding up in the Google Graveyard.5 The Logos that defines Google's technology foundation doesn't necessarily benefit the company's shareholders, and corporations do not have artistic integrity.

For Facebook, it's the Graph API. Taking your personal information and relationships and deconstructing them into into an easily traversable map of information snippets to manipulate users, similar to how a virus uses our own building blocks to attack us. Never underestimate a nerd with self esteem issues can accomplish when he puts his mind to it. For Beethoven, it was composing Für Elise. For Zuckerberg, it's the creation of an endless stream of reality warping dystopia machines designed to feed his hunger for endless profits by openly creating a reality that farms hate based on what has been called the "Zuck Curve.". (See A Blueprint For Content Governance And Enforcement Facebook.)

Genius does not select its path... genius doesn't have an alignment... but it must have elegance. The problem with these technologies is that they are fueled by turning their customers into their product, creating a kind of toxic feedback loop that magnifies the worse in human interaction.

For me, programming is a creative, artistic practice. When I code, I am trying to find that sweet spot in a system where its fundamental elements can flower like this. I know, this is a quest of folly, but I find that it creates systems that are much easier to build and maintain.

The Power of One

This book is an exploration of the self, and how it explores and creates. It is a testament to my love of the creative side of programming.

One of the sayings I coined was an orchestra never wrote a symphony. I believe that in software just as much as I do in music. There is a beautiful harmony in people coming together to manifest someone's dream. But let's not forget that the dreamers need space to dream. It is the partnership that defines civilization. The one inspiring the many, and the many creating the space for many ones.

I am not saying I am a genius. A great friend once said to me, "you are a seeker." A truer word about me was never said. I seek a world that gives space to every seeker, and a platform for each to have their moments. This does not place us above others, just acknowledges the power of creation while bowing in eternal gratitude to the collective coming together to build those visions. Not for profit of self, but for upliftingof all.
